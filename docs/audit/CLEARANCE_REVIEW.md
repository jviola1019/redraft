## Plan validation and study results

1. **Separate structural replay, retrospective prediction and prospective validation.** The fresh matrix completed 864 drafts, 1,728 paired replicates and 288 deterministic replays, across 32 teams and nine classes. The playoff reconstruction matched the checked qualifiers and wildcard/divisional fixtures for 2018–2025. The independent integrity check ran 18 paired draft comparisons and returned zero failures. Combine-based playable pools and unequal historical reference pools still limit interpretation. Personnel information requires contemporaneous sources even when simulated standings reconcile.
2. **Use every completed outcome and preserve censoring.** There are five complete observation years each for draft origins 2018–2021, then four, three, two, one and zero for 2022–2026. Across all nine origins, 13 of 30 evaluable origin-season cells missed at least one conditional 95% range across the tracked measures. By origin, affected/evaluable cells were 3/5, 2/5, 2/5, 2/5, 2/4, 1/3, 1/2, 0/1 and 0/0. Fifteen of 45 cells have incomplete or future outcomes. Overlapping actual seasons and multiple tested metrics prevent interpreting 13/30 as an independent error probability. The all-year diagnostic counts shutout games; the four-scenario diagnostic above counts zero team scores.
3. **Prioritize the later-origin QB alternative.** On 50 QBs from 2019–2021, recruiting all then-mature donor cohorts reduced CRPS from 862.21 to 800.14. The paired difference was −62.07 (within-origin bootstrap 95% interval −115.85 to −12.39). Coverage rose from 39/50 to 49/50, mean interval width from 2,029 to 5,217 snaps, and MAE worsened from 1,086 to 1,209. This was an unconditional experiment on three previously seen classes. Compare its production-conditioned version with persistence and position baselines under a frozen protocol. The 2016–17 annual-transition hindcast gave a five-year CRPS difference of −2.88 (−38.36 to +29.61). The age-rank alternative also failed its adoption criterion. Keep all of these results in the record.
4. **Rebuild game-scoring validation around chronology and dependence.** The discrete marginal candidate improved total-score CRPS from 7.77349 to 7.75749 in 2016–17, but shutout Brier worsened from 0.026638 to 0.026765. The empirical score-pair candidate lost team-season variance. Both results argue for testing a shared team-strength and possession model. nflreadr documents play-by-play since 1999, providing a feasible source to investigate; drive quality, licensing and seasonal completeness still need to be verified before use.
5. **Benchmark playoff prediction separately from bracket correctness.** We regenerated 960 forecasts across 256 distinct observed team-seasons, with 64 draws per franchise/origin/season. Overall Brier was 0.24016 versus 0.24492 for slot prevalence. At one year it was 0.22521 versus 0.24316; at five years 0.24672 versus 0.24609. The horizon-five Monte Carlo standard error was 0.00567. The difference at that horizon is small relative to simulation noise and fails to establish five-year skill. A plausible stronger benchmark uses only pre-origin team strength. For a future preregistered assessment, report per-horizon skill and season-level uncertainty and disclose schedule and rule hindsight.
6. **Interpret the leaderboard as a game rubric.** Four fixed strategies on one CLE scenario produced two leader-set changes under single-weight perturbations, with lowest tied Spearman correlation 0.333. Test multiple dates, franchises and decisions under predeclared weight families. Report tied-leader changes and ranking stability by scenario. No human skill validation is available. The CPU change increased first-round QBs from one to 72 across 108 drafts, still well below the historical rate of 31 in nine actual drafts. The 108 runs repeat those same nine historical environments.
7. **Keep college hidden on competitive blind boards.** College plus position uniquely identifies 2,140 of 2,944 prospects across the nine initial boards in the saved uniqueness audit. This measures database identifiability. A timed user recognition study with novices and experienced fans would measure actual recognition and scouting utility. Showing personal identities after the embargo is a separate game feature.

## Evaluator and provenance repairs

Three modified evidence fixtures reproduced permissive behavior before editing. A favorable confidence interval from the reused cohort could clear the individual-skill gate. Removing a forecast hash removed its check. Removing the CPU draft-policy hash still passed simulation freshness. The repaired evaluator requires the seven forecast inputs, verifies the CPU policy hash, and classifies evidence from this reused-cohort evaluator as insufficient for independent validation. Four regressions cover these cases, including omission of the complete forecast manifest. All 12 statistical-audit tests passed. A read-only reviewer checked the measurements and ran the first three regression cases independently. The seven required forecast inputs and current simulation list cover named dependencies; this is not an exhaustive provenance proof for arbitrary future code changes.

The separate annual QB transition suite detected two stale source hashes (forecast evaluator code and raw games). Re-executing its experiment from these inputs changed only those two hashes in the saved research artifact. All six transition tests then passed; the substantive five-year finding did not change. This catches a provenance issue outside the 53 formal checks. Two first attempts at running supplemental tests needed corrected Python import and output paths; the corrected commands passed. The projection, ranking and source tests assess different hypotheses. No test establishes that an unobserved future season happened.
