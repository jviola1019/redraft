# Discrete score marginal recalibration protocol

Freeze one candidate before executing new comparative results. The audited runtime uses correlated Gaussian total and margin innovations, shared team-season effects, and rounded scores. The candidate retains the identical latent total and margin and calibrates the two latent score marginals separately to empirical pre-cutoff home and away score distributions. Calibrate using the already selected three-season lookback. Apply the empirical marginal quantile at the latent score's rank in the model-implied marginal distribution, which integrates the season effect and the historical matchup mix. Draw no independent score innovations. Resolve ties with the existing regular-season overtime rule.

Each rolling origin in 2013–2017 uses only completed preceding seasons. Compare 2013–2015 and 2016–2017 separately, using common random draws and unchanged correlated-normal controls. The previously examined 2018–2022 full five-season replay is a secondary diagnostic if the rolling evidence supports adoption. Do not use it for model selection. The primary outputs are combined, signed margin, home and away CRPS, mean bias, MAE and central 90% coverage, plus shutout Brier and frequency. Simulate joint scheduled seasons for those same rolling origins and compare win and points dispersion and team season intraclass residual dependence. Publish all windows.

Promotion requires lower combined-score CRPS and lower shutout Brier in both windows, no greater than 1% degradation in home, away or margin CRPS or any MAE, no more than a one percentage point increase in absolute coverage error in any metric, maintained shared-team dependence, and no large divergence from the baseline in team win or points dispersion (relative change above 5%). Promotion also requires a direct versioned runtime counterpart and full engine checks. These engineering criteria apply to this candidate only and leave all statistical release thresholds untouched. All rolling assessment outcomes were previously exposed.

## Fixture-validation prompt

Given a synthetic completed schedule before an origin and arbitrary games at and after it, check that modifying future scores leaves training parameters and forecasts at the origin unchanged. Verify that shared teams get one strength shock per replicate across games, reciprocal fixtures get separate game draws, and baseline and candidate use the same latent draws. Verify that score quantiles are monotonic, emitted scores are nonnegative integers, ordinary scoring excludes one-point team totals, and shutouts have finite positive support. Independently recompute metric arithmetic on small hand-calculable arrays and check that monthly or season endpoint observations cannot affect earlier predictions.
